Unsupervised Learning - Concepts

Unsupervised learning is a branch of Machine Learning where the algorithm learns patterns from unlabeled data. Unlike supervised learning, there is no target variable to predict.

Supervised vs Unsupervised
Supervised
  • Labeled data
  • Predicts a known target
  • Classification or regression
  • Example: exam pass/fail
Unsupervised
  • Unlabeled data
  • Discovers hidden structure
  • Clustering or dimensionality reduction
  • Example: used-car grouping
What is Clustering?

Clustering groups similar records together based on distance in the feature space. Records within the same cluster are more similar to each other than to records in other clusters.

What is K-Means?

K-Means is a clustering algorithm that partitions the data into K groups. Each group is represented by a centroid - the mean position of the points assigned to that group.

How K-Means Works
  1. Select K initial centroids.
  2. Assign each record to the nearest centroid using Euclidean distance.
  3. Recalculate each centroid as the mean of its assigned records.
  4. Repeat steps 2 and 3 until the centroids stop changing (convergence).
Euclidean Distance
d = sqrt((x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2)
Why Scaling Matters

Vehicle age ranges around 0-30, while mileage ranges around 0-300,000. Without scaling, mileage would dominate every distance calculation. We apply StandardScaler to bring both variables to a comparable scale.

Key Metrics
  • Within-cluster variance (inertia): sum of squared distances from each point to its centroid. Lower is better.
  • Silhouette score: ranges from -1 to 1. Higher values indicate better separation and cohesion.
Contextualization - Used Car Market

The used-car market contains vehicles with very different usage profiles. A car that is 2 years old with 15,000 miles is fundamentally different from a 12-year-old car with 180,000 miles. Dealers, analysts and buyers can benefit from understanding these natural groups.

Using only vehicle age and mileage, K-Means can identify groupings such as newer low-mileage vehicles, intermediate vehicles, and older high-mileage vehicles.

However, two variables cannot capture the full condition of a vehicle. Factors like accident history, service records, and brand reliability also matter.
